# Pitch Speech — Dataset, Narrowing, and KPIs

A speaking script, one beat at a time. Say it in your own words, but hit the numbers in **bold** exactly — they're all verified in `docs/cohort_definition.md`, `docs/research_and_measurement_plan.md`, and `docs/key_research_indicators.md`.

---

## 1. Open with the question

> "We wanted to answer one question: **what stops salaried Gen Z from investing in mutual funds after they've already thought about it** — and what would actually change their mind? Not 'why don't people invest' in general — specifically, people who got as far as *considering* it and then didn't pull the trigger. That gap is where INDmoney can actually do something."

---

## 2. Introduce the dataset

> "We didn't run our own survey. We used the **SEBI Investor Survey 2025** — a national respondent-level dataset, **109,430 respondents** in total. It's a government survey, so it's not built for INDmoney specifically, but it's large, credible, and asks the right questions: what people hold, what they've considered, why they haven't invested, and what would encourage them."

**Pause here.** This sets up why the next section — narrowing — is necessary: the raw dataset is enormous and mostly irrelevant to the question.

---

## 3. Walk through the narrowing, step by step

Say this as a funnel — each step should feel like closing in on the target audience, not a technical filter.

> "Here's how we got from **109,430 people to the exact 553 we actually study.**

1. "First, we only kept people who completed the full survey — not just a short screening stage. That's **53,357** people.
2. "Next, we kept only **Gen Z** — ages 18 to 28, per SEBI's own age bands. That's **24,576**.
3. "Then we kept only people who are **salaried** — we didn't guess at this; we went to SEBI's own official occupation classification and matched it exactly, category by category. That brings us to **4,346** salaried Gen Z respondents. This is our *broader group.*
4. "From that broader group, we asked: who has actually **considered mutual funds** — said yes, I've thought about this — but **does not currently hold one**? That's the intersection we actually care about, and it comes out to **553 people**. This is our *focused group* — the heart of the whole analysis."

> "So: 109,000-plus respondents nationally, narrowed down to 553 people who match our exact target — salaried Gen Z, seriously considered mutual funds, haven't invested yet."

---

## 4. Pre-empt the obvious question: "is 553 too small / too narrow?"

> "A couple of things we were careful about. One — this group isn't uniform. Within these 553, **136 have actually invested in mutual funds before and stopped** — they're not blank-slate. **382 report no prior investment** in any major securities product. And **35 have invested elsewhere but never in mutual funds.** So when we talk about barriers, we can split them by this experience, not treat them as one crowd.

> "Two — every number from here on is **unweighted** and describes *this sample*, not the whole country. We're not claiming this is India's Gen Z. We're claiming: within this specific, carefully-defined group of near-miss investors, here's what they told us."

---

## 5. Now pitch the KPIs — one at a time

> "From here, everything we measure follows one simple rule: **take the group who actually answered a question, count who picked each option, divide, done.** No modeling, no assumptions filled in for blanks — a blank stays a blank."

Walk through each KPI group like a menu, using the headline number as the hook:

1. **Motivations** — *"Why are they even considering it?"* → top reason: long-term wealth building, selected by about a quarter of answerers.
2. **Barriers** — *"What's actually stopping them?"* → the single biggest one: **fear of losing money to market risk, cited by 30.5%** of the people who answered this question.
3. **Encouragement factors** — *"What would move them?"* → the top answer: **a simpler investing process — 44% said this would help.** That's a product problem, not a market problem.
4. **Previous experience** — a reminder that this isn't a blank-slate audience; nearly a quarter have tried mutual funds before and stopped.
5. **Group differences** — *"Does this change by income or experience?"* → yes: fear of losing money **rises with income**, from about 26% at the lowest tier to over 42% at the highest. And people with **no prior investment ask for education twice as often** as past investors do.

> "Put together: the biggest barrier is fear of loss, especially among higher earners — and the biggest fix people are asking for is a simpler process, especially for people with no investing history at all."

---

## 6. Land the pitch — what this means for INDmoney

> "This gives us two clear, testable directions:
> - **Simplify onboarding** — because 'simple and easy process' is the single most-requested encouragement factor.
> - **Address risk perception directly**, especially for higher-income segments, since fear of loss is not evenly spread — it's concentrated exactly where the money is.

> "We're not claiming this data proves either idea will work — SEBI's survey can't see INDmoney's app, its funnel, or its conversions. What it gives us is a **validated, narrowed-down, real signal** of where to test first, instead of guessing."

---

## 7. Close

> "So: from 109,000+ respondents, we found the 553 people who matter most to this question, we know exactly how experienced they are, we know what's stopping them and what would help — and we have two concrete product bets to test next."
